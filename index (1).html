Who owns the market, and who moves it

On a typical day, only about 1.7% of the market's value changes hands. That thin slice set the price for everything else. Source: S&P 500, FRED, St. Louis Fed.

“In the short run, the market is a voting machine but in the long run it is a weighing machine.”

Benjamin Graham, as quoted by Warren Buffett in his letters to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders

Share of all US stock, by household wealth

Line up every US household from richest to poorest. How much of the stock market does each group own?

  • The wealthiest 1% of households own about 50% of all stock
  • The next wealthiest 9% of households own about 37%
  • All other households (the least wealthy 90%) own about 13% combined

Households ranked by net worth, not yearly income. Approximate, based on Federal Reserve distributional data. In plain terms: the bottom nine out of ten households together own about 13 cents of every dollar of stock.

Share of all US stock, by type of account

The same market, sliced by where the stock is held rather than by who is wealthy.

  • 401(k)s ~10%
  • IRAs and pensions ~27%
  • Taxable accounts ~24%
  • Foreign investors ~25%
  • Insurers, nonprofits, other ~14%

Retirement accounts total about 37% (Tax Policy Center, 2015 data). The 401(k) split is a rough estimate. These accounts belong mostly to the wealthiest 10% of households, so this bar overlaps the one above rather than adding to it.

Share of all consumer spending, by household income

Who keeps company revenues, and therefore earnings, growing?

  • The highest-earning 10% of households do about 50% of all spending
  • All other households (the lower-earning 90%) do the other 50% combined

Ranked by yearly income, not net worth (Moody's Analytics, 2025). This feeds a loop: stock gains make wealthy households feel richer, they spend more, earnings rise, and stocks rise again. The loop also runs in reverse.

Share of the market's value traded in one day

On any given day, almost all stock sits still. The last trade still reprices every share.

Average day, 2025: about $1.1 trillion traded

Best day: April 9, 2025. S&P 500 up about 9.5% on a tariff pause, record $1.86 trillion traded

Estimates: dollars traded divided by approximate total market value that day. The April 4 dollar figure is estimated from share volume.

Who does the trading on an average day

Most trading comes from computers, and they end the day owning almost nothing.

High-frequency trading (HFT) firms such as Citadel Securities and Virtu use algorithms to trade the same shares thousands of times a second. Many retail orders from apps like Robinhood are executed by these firms through payment for order flow.

2026: which stocks drive the most dollars traded?

A handful of names take an outsized share of every trading dollar.

Share of all US dollar trading volume, 2026 year to date

  • Nvidia 3.0%
  • Micron 2.8%
  • Tesla 2.1%
  • Next 7 most-traded ~8.5%
  • Roughly 4,000 other stocks ~84%

The top 10 stocks together account for about 16.4% of every dollar traded (Goldman Sachs). Three companies alone take about 8 cents of every trading dollar. Micron trades far more than its size would suggest.

What it adds up to

A large, sticky base of ownership, mostly the wealthiest households and their retirement accounts, rarely sells. On top of it sits a thin, hyperactive layer that does most of the trading and sets prices at the margin. That is why prices can swing by trillions of dollars in a day while ownership barely changes.
