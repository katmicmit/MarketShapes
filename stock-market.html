Who owns the US stock market

Share of the total value of publicly traded US stock, with US households lined up from richest to poorest.

  • Foreign investors, based outside the US, own about 34%
  • The wealthiest 1% of US households own about 32%
  • The next wealthiest 9% own about 23%
  • The middle 40% own about 7%
  • Nonprofits, endowments and insurers own about 4%
  • The remaining US households, the least wealthy half, own about 0.4%

Foreign and institutional shares: Tax Policy Center, 2022 data. Household groups apply the Federal Reserve's Q2 2026 wealth split to the roughly 62% of publicly traded stock that US households own. Two sources are combined, so treat the shares as approximate. In plain terms: foreign investors own about as much of the US market as America's wealthiest 1%, while half of US households together own less than half a cent of every dollar, even though 58% of families own at least some stock.

Share of all consumer spending, by household income

Who keeps company revenues, and therefore earnings, growing?

  • The highest-earning 10% of households do about 50% of all spending
  • All other households (the lower-earning 90%) do the other 50% combined

Ranked by yearly income, not net worth (Moody's Analytics, 2025). This feeds a loop: stock gains make wealthy households feel richer, they spend more, earnings rise, and stocks rise again. The loop also runs in reverse.

What it adds up to

A large, sticky base of ownership, mostly the wealthiest households and their retirement accounts, rarely sells. On top of it sits a thin, hyperactive layer that does most of the trading and sets prices at the margin. That is why prices can swing by trillions of dollars in a day while ownership barely changes.
